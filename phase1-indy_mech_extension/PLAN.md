# indy_mech_extension — can a linear probe on Qwen3-8B's hidden states predict (1) assistant vs not, (2) broken text vs not?

**Status (2026-09-09, end of day): DONE — see `README.md` for results.** Deviations from this plan: LDA dropped (too slow at 4096-d); logistic at 5 layers only; two controls added after review (bag-of-tokens baseline, no-prompt rerun); phase 19 features extracted but by decision not judged or scored; trigger-identity probes added for debiasing; label shuffle ran 10 permutations, not 20 (found 2026-09-12).

## What phase 17 leaves on the table

Phase 17 (and its `hbar-rerun` extension) put 16 junk tokens in front of `what shall i do today` and
sampled Qwen3-8B 24 times per trigger at T=1.0, 96 new tokens. Blind judges then read every
rollout. The finding that survived every instrument: clean and random-prefix nulls are **0%
persona**, optimised triggers run **15–70%** persona, and *the same trigger* gives a default
assistant reply on one seed and a Korean gamer on the next. Nothing in phase 17 looks inside the
model. This extension asks: **is "I am about to answer as somebody else" linearly readable from the
residual stream, and how early?**

|---|---|---|

| trigger token ids per arm | same file, `arms[*].ids` | 22 arms |
| blind judgments, panel A (full sweep, 2 coders/rollout) | `phase17/hbar-rerun/judging_full/` + `corpus_key_full.json` | 1,056 |
| blind judgments, panel B (phase 17 §14 wide panel, 2 coders/rollout) | `phase17/judging2/` + `_key.json` | 1,056 |

Four independent blind coders per rollout, each giving `default_assistant`, `persona`, and
`coherent` (2 fluent / 1 partly broken / 0 degenerate). **Two binary targets**, each at two
strictness tiers. Counted locally 2026-09-09:

**Target 1 — `assistant` vs `not_assistant`**

| tier | definition | positives / negatives |
|---|---|---|
| **1A unanimous** | all 4 coders `default_assistant` vs all 4 `persona`; mixed and broken dropped | 226 / 144 (n=370) |
| **1B full-sweep** | both full-sweep coders `default_assistant` vs everything else (persona *or* broken) | 240 / 288 (n=528) |

**Target 2 — `broken_text` vs `not_broken`**

| tier | definition | broken / not broken |
|---|---|---|
| **2A unanimous** | all 4 coders `coherent ≤ 1` vs all 4 `coherent = 2`; mixed dropped | 59 / 312 (n=371) |
| **2B majority-ish** | at least 2 of 4 coders `coherent ≤ 1` vs fewer than 2 | 153 / 375 (n=528) |

Of the 59 unanimously broken rollouts, 29 were scored fully degenerate (0) by all four coders and
30 partly broken (1). Both nulls are fluent on 47/48; one NULL-random rollout got a single 1.

⚠ **The two targets are entangled.** 53 of the 59 unanimously broken rollouts are also
unanimously *not* assistant, so an "assistant" probe can score well simply by detecting broken
text. Two extra runs separate them:

- assistant probe restricted to **unanimously fluent** rollouts only (180 assistant / 85 not);
- broken probe restricted to **unanimously not-assistant** rollouts only (53 broken / 85 fluent).

If a probe direction survives both restrictions it is reading two different things; if the
assistant probe collapses on fluent-only data, it was a brokenness detector.

The 48 null rollouts are 48/48 assistant and 47/48 fluent in every tier; results are reported
**with and without nulls**, because nulls are trivially separable and would flatter any probe.

Per-arm rates are far from uniform (e.g. `s909-random` 16 default / 3 persona; `s101-random`
2 / 16), so a probe could "cheat" by recognising the trigger. That is handled by the split below.

## Method

### 1. Rebuild the exact token sequence for each rollout

Rig = `hbar-rerun/hbar_personas.ipynb` cell 1, byte-for-byte (same chat template with
`enable_thinking=False`, same offset-based scaffold split `PRE_P + trigger + SUF_P`, same
`gen()`). Each stored rollout has `text`, `first_id`, `n`, `seed`. Re-tokenise `text`; accept if
first id and length match (462/528 did last time). Otherwise regenerate from the stored seed
(66/528; deterministic, 0 failures last time). Assert 0 failures before extracting anything.

### 2. One teacher-forced forward pass per rollout, `output_hidden_states=True`

Input = prompt ids + response ids (~33 + ≤96 tokens). Causal attention means the hidden state at
position *t* is exactly what the model had when it sampled token *t+1*, so this reproduces the
generation-time internals with no sampling loop.

**Layers:** every 2nd layer, 0 (embeddings) to 36 (final) → **19 layers** of the 4096-dim
residual stream.

**Positions stored per layer** (10 slots):

| slot | meaning |
|---|---|
| `P` | last prompt token — the state that produced response token 1. Identical for all 24 seeds of an arm, so this slot can only carry *per-trigger* information. It is the "before anything is sampled" baseline. |
| `R1, R2, R4, R8, R16, R32, R64` | state after *k* response tokens have been committed |
| `Rmean` | mean over all response positions |
| `Rlast` | final response token |

Stored as fp16: 528 × 19 × 10 × 4096 × 2 B ≈ **0.8 GB**. Saved to `/content` and Google Drive
(user mounts), and pulled locally if the transfer is reasonable; all probe training runs on CPU
so nothing after step 2 needs a GPU.

### 3. Probes, per (layer, slot)

- **Mass-mean**: direction = mean(class 1) − mean(class 0) on the training fold; score = projection;
  threshold fitted on the training fold. Also the whitened variant (Σ⁻¹·Δμ, shrinkage covariance),
  reported alongside.
- **Logistic regression**: standardised features, L2, C picked by inner 3-fold CV on the training
  fold only.

### 4. Evaluation — the split is the whole game

- **Primary: leave-one-trigger-out.** 22 folds, each holding out every rollout of one arm.
  Metric: AUROC and balanced accuracy, pooled over folds. This is the only split that answers
  "does the probe read the *state*, or just recognise which trigger is in the prompt?"
- **Control 1: random 5-fold split** (arms mixed across folds). The gap between this and
  leave-one-trigger-out measures how much of the score was trigger recognition.
- **Control 2: per-arm base-rate predictor** — predict each rollout by its own arm's training
  persona rate. This is the ceiling for anything that lives in slot `P`.
- **Control 3: label-shuffle** within arm, 20 permutations, to get a chance band per (layer, slot).
- Sanity: slot `P` under leave-one-trigger-out should sit near chance on tier A minus nulls, and
  near control 2 on random splits. If it does not, something leaked.

### 5. Outputs

- `probe_persona.ipynb` — the Colab notebook, executed, outputs kept.
- `features_qwen_wide.npz` — the hidden-state tensor (or a Drive link if too large).
- `labels.json` — per-rollout labels for both targets at both tiers, with all four coder verdicts.
- `results.json` + `README.md` — AUROC/accuracy grids: 19 layers × 10 slots × 2 probes × 2 targets
  × 2 tiers × 2 splits, plus the three controls and the two disentangling restrictions. Two headline plots per target: best-layer AUROC vs. response
  position (how early is it readable?), and AUROC vs. layer at the best position.

## Risks and what I will do about them

- **Regen mismatch on a different GPU / transformers version.** Assert first_id and length; abort
  and report rather than silently include a wrong sequence.
- **`output_hidden_states` memory.** 37 × 130 × 4096 × 2 B ≈ 39 MB per pass; not an issue.
- **Class imbalance.** Tier 1B without nulls is 192 / 288; tier 2A is 59 / 312 and the
  not-assistant-only broken run is 53 / 85. Use AUROC and balanced accuracy, never raw accuracy,
  and class-weight the logistic probe. Under leave-one-trigger-out some folds will hold out an arm
  with zero broken rollouts; AUROC is pooled across folds, not averaged, for exactly this reason.
- **Judge labels are panel-conditional (phase 17 §14).** That is why tier A demands 4/4 agreement
  across two panels that share no members.
- **n is small.** 370 or 528 examples against 4096 dims: mass-mean and strongly regularised
  logistic only, and every number carries a shuffle band.
